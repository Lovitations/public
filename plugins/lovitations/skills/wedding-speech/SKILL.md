# Wedding Speech

A good wedding speech is short, specific and sounds like the person holding
the microphone. It tells one or two real stories, says something true about
the couple and ends with a toast. Two ways to get it wrong: generic lines that
fit any couple, and an invented anecdote the guests know never happened.

## Ground rules

- Stories, names and details come from the speaker. Nothing is invented; if
  there is not enough material, ask for it.
- No jokes about exes, money or anything the speaker says is sensitive. When
  in doubt, ask.
- Time it: about 130 spoken words per minute. Ask how long they have; default
  to three minutes.
- Answer in the language the user writes in. If the guests speak two
  languages, offer lines in both.

## When to use

"Write my speech", "toast for my brother's wedding", "I'm the best man",
"speech voor de bruiloft van mijn zus", "toespraak als getuige", "discurso
para la boda de mi hermana", "Rede als Trauzeuge".

## Steps

1. **Ask who is speaking and to whom:** their role, their relation to the
   couple, the audience, how long, the tone (moving, funny, both).
2. **Ask for material:** two or three stories, how they met the couple, what
   they love about the pair, words or habits that are typically theirs.
3. **Pick the thread**: one idea that ties the stories together.
4. **Write it** in spoken language: short sentences, a clear opening, one or
   two stories, what this says about the couple, a toast.
5. **Time it** and show the word count. Mark where to pause.
6. **Offer two alternatives** for the opening line and for the toast.

## Output format

```markdown
# Speech for Emma & Daan (about 3 minutes, 390 words)

For those who don't know me: I'm Lisa, Emma's sister, and for 28 years
the person who had to wait outside the bathroom. (pause)

...

So please raise your glasses. To Emma and Daan!

**Other openings:** ...
**Check:** is the camping story OK to tell with grandma in the room?
```

## Limits

- The speech is a draft in the speaker's words; they should read it aloud once
  and change what does not sound like them.
